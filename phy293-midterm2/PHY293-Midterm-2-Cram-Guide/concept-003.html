Probability density and interval probability

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Foundations

Why this matters

Turn the wavefunction into measurable probabilities.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A wavefunction is a probability amplitude. The density ρ(x)=|Ψ(x)|2 tells you probability per unit length. A small region dx has probability approximately ρ(x)dx.

Assumptions

A normalized one-dimensional state obeys ∫|Ψ|2dx=1. Its wavefunction has units length to the power −1/2; its density has units length to the power −1.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · P(a≤x≤b)=∫ab|Ψ(x)|2dx. Intersect the requested interval with the support of the state. A density may be greater than 1 in chosen units; a probability must lie between 0 and 1. For a continuous position distribution, the probability of one exact point is zero.

Example

Level 3 · If ρ=1/L on [0,L], then P(L/4≤x≤L/2)=1/4. If the question asks for [−L/4,L/2], the portion outside the support contributes zero, so the answer is 1/2.
Common trap: The most likely position maximizes the density, not a signed or complex wavefunction.

PROBLEM TYPE

5. Normalize a shape and calculate probabilities

Level 1 · A wavefunction is a probability amplitude. The density ρ(x)=|Ψ(x)|2 tells you probability per unit length. A small region dx has probability approximately ρ(x)dx.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · P(a≤x≤b)=∫ab|Ψ(x)|2dx. Intersect the requested interval with the support of the state. A density may be greater than 1 in chosen units; a probability must lie between 0 and 1. For a continuous position distribution, the probability of one exact point is zero.

Worked example

PS8 Q4: normalize ψ=Ax(L−x) on 0≤x≤L, then find left-half probability and mean position.

  1. ∫0Lx2(L−x)2dx=L5/30, so A=30/L5/2.
  2. The density is symmetric about L/2. Equal halves give P(0≤x≤L/2)=1/2.
  3. The same symmetry gives ⟨x⟩=L/2. This shape is admissible as a state but is not a single box energy eigenfunction.
