Fourier transforms and localized packets

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Wave packets

Why this matters

Localization requires a range of wavenumbers.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A single plane wave extends everywhere with constant density. A superposition of nearby wavenumbers can interfere constructively in a localized region and destructively elsewhere.

Assumptions

Use one transform convention. Here the symmetric quantum convention is used; the lecture also shows a classical convention with 1/(2π) in only one transform. A plane wave is an idealized momentum/scattering state, not a normalizable localized state on the entire real line.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Ψ(x,0)=12π∫ϕ(k)eikxdk,ϕ(k)=12π∫Ψ(x,0)e−ikxdx. A constant k-amplitude over bandwidth B produces an envelope proportional to sin(Bx/2)/x, with first zeros at x=±2π/B. Narrowing the spatial packet requires broadening its k spectrum.

Example

Level 3 · Doubling B halves the distance to the first zero. This first-zero width is not a standard deviation; do not use it as σx without justification. An ideal rectangular k spectrum can give a spatial variance that diverges.
Common trap: Different Fourier normalization conventions are valid; mixing their prefactors is not.

Worked example

PS8 Q1: find coefficients for a centered unit pulse of width a repeated every P.

  1. Even symmetry gives Bn=0. Average value gives A0/2=a/P.
  2. An=(2/P)∫−a/2a/2cos(2πnx/P)dx.
  3. Integration yields An=2sin(nπa/P)/(nπ). Harmonics vanish when n a/P is an integer. A narrow pulse generally requires more high-frequency components.
