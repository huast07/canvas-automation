Surface temperatures and dominant resistance

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Recover interface temperatures and identify where the largest drop occurs.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

After finding heat rate, recover each surface or interface temperature by subtracting the temperature drop across each resistance along the direction of heat flow.

Assumptions

Use the local branch heat rate. Temperatures lie between hot and cold reservoirs in a passive network with no source; this check can fail if a source is present.

Reasoning / derivation

From ΔTi=Q˙Ri, Tnext=Tprevious−Q˙Ri. The fraction of the total drop is Ri/Rtotal. An interface beside a very small resistance is close in temperature to the other end of that resistance.

Example

In the preceding original wall example, inner surface 20−114.286(0.05)=14.286 ∘C; outer surface 0+114.286(0.025)=2.857 ∘C. Drops 5.714+11.429+2.857 sum to 20 K.

PROBLEM TYPE

2 · Composite wall and surface temperatures

After finding heat rate, recover each surface or interface temperature by subtracting the temperature drop across each resistance along the direction of heat flow.

Reasoning / derivation

From ΔTi=Q˙Ri, Tnext=Tprevious−Q˙Ri. The fraction of the total drop is Ri/Rtotal. An interface beside a very small resistance is close in temperature to the other end of that resistance.

Worked example

Original example: 20°C indoor air and 0°C outdoor air; area 2 m²; wall thickness 0.1 m, k=0.5 W/(m K); inside h=10 and outside h=20 W/(m² K). Ignore radiation and contact resistance.

  1. Ri=1/(10×2)=0.05, Rw=0.1/(0.5×2)=0.1, Ro=1/(20×2)=0.025 K/W.
  2. Rtotal=0.175 K/W; Q˙=20/0.175=114.286 W.
  3. Ts,i=20−114.286(0.05)=14.286 ∘C.
  4. Ts,o=0+114.286(0.025)=2.857 ∘C. The drop across the wall is 11.429 K, which reproduces the same heat rate by Fourier law.
