Condensation threshold and insulation thickness

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Design problems

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Turn a dew-point requirement into an insulation-thickness equation.

Scope: inferred

From first principles

A cold exposed surface can collect condensation when its temperature is below the air dew point. Insulation design can set the outer surface at the limiting dew-point temperature.

Assumptions

Steady network; given ambient dew point; use consistent combined or separate boundary coefficients. At the limiting design, surface temperature equals dew point; a design margin needs additional thickness analysis.

Reasoning / derivation

For a cold insulated pipe at threshold, Q˙in=ho(2πroL)(T∞−Tdew). The same inward heat rate crosses the insulation, pipe and inner film. Set this equal to (Tdew−Tfluid)/Rinside path. Radius affects both logarithmic insulation resistance and exposed area, so solve the nonlinear equation for outer radius; thickness is outer radius minus bare-pipe outer radius.

Example

Original substep: T∞=20 ∘C, Tdew=10 ∘C, ho=20, ro=0.04 m, L=1 give threshold inward heat rate 50.2655 W. This alone does not determine thickness; the internal path must also match.

Worked example

Current review Problem 3: wire diameter 2.2 mm, length 14 m, 1 mm plastic insulation, k=0.15 W/(m K), current 13 A, voltage drop 8 V, ambient 30°C, h=24 W/(m² K). Find interface temperature and effect of doubling insulation thickness.

  1. P=VI=104 W, ri=0.0011 m, ro=0.0021 m.
  2. R=ln(ro/ri)/(2πkL)+1/(2πroLh). Then Ti=30+104R. The independently calculated result is given in the verification note below. Interface temperature: 58.555°C initially and 54.057°C after doubling.
  3. Doubling insulation thickness makes ro=0.0031 m, not 0.0042 m. Both outer radii are below k/h=0.00625 m.
  4. Increasing radius toward the critical radius reduces total resistance and thus lowers wire temperature at fixed 104 W. The printed 6.25 mm answer is a radius, not a temperature.
