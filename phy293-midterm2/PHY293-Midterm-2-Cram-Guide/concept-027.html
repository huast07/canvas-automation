Boundary conditions and admissible states

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Quantum dynamics

Why this matters

Use the potential and the domain to decide which mathematical solutions are physical.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · The differential equation has many solutions; boundary conditions select the ones compatible with confinement and finite probability.

Assumptions

At a finite potential step with constant mass and no delta-function singularity, both ψ and its first derivative are continuous. An infinite wall is a different limit: ψ vanishes there; derivative continuity with the exterior is not required.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Integrate TISE across a shrinking interval around a finite step. The integral of (U−E)ψ tends to zero when finite, so ψ′(0+)−ψ′(0−)=0. A jump in ψ would introduce a stronger singularity. At spatial infinity retain only decaying terms for a bound state.

Example

Level 3 · For a right-hand forbidden region x>L, use Ce−κ(x−L), not Ce+κ(x−L). At a left-hand infinity the decaying sign reverses: Aeκx decays as x→−∞.
Common trap: Do not impose ψ=0 at a finite barrier; penetration into the forbidden region is possible.

Worked example

2025 QIII route: write the bound-state forms for the asymmetric 0.4 eV / 0 / 0.2 eV well of width L=10 nm, using constant effective mass m*=0.067me and 0<E<0.2 eV.

  1. Left: AeκLx; interior: Bcoskx+Csinkx; right: De−κR(x−L).
  2. k=2m*E/ℏ, κL=2m*(0.4eV−E)/ℏ, and κR=2m*(0.2eV−E)/ℏ. Convert eV to joules if inserting SI constants.
  3. At 0: A=B, κLA=kC. At L: BcoskL+CsinkL=D and −kBsinkL+kCcoskL=−κRD.
  4. These are the requested matching equations. This historical extension is included for method practice; confirm its precise scope against forthcoming Lecture 2-11.
