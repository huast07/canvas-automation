Parallel heat paths

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Combine alternative paths sharing the same endpoint temperatures.

Scope: inferred

From first principles

Parallel branches share the same endpoint temperatures while their heat rates add. This models conducting screws, copper vias, or different building surfaces.

Assumptions

A simple parallel network assumes appropriate isothermal endpoint planes and approximately independent 1D paths. Real spreading near screws can require a multidimensional model.

Reasoning / derivation

For each branch Q˙i=ΔT/Ri. Therefore 1Req=∑i1Ri. For parallel materials of equal length, keff=∑ikiAi/A. Partial-depth screws require the parallel section in series with the remaining wood under the stated isothermal-plane approximation.

Example

Original example: R1=2, R2=3 K/W, ΔT=12 K. Branch rates are 6 W and 4 W, total 10 W, and Req=1.2 K/W.

PROBLEM TYPE

3 · Parallel and mixed networks

Parallel branches share the same endpoint temperatures while their heat rates add. This models conducting screws, copper vias, or different building surfaces.

Reasoning / derivation

For each branch Q˙i=ΔT/Ri. Therefore 1Req=∑i1Ri. For parallel materials of equal length, keff=∑ikiAi/A. Partial-depth screws require the parallel section in series with the remaining wood under the stated isothermal-plane approximation.

Worked example

Original example: two paths of 2 and 3 K/W connect a hot node to an interface. A shared outer film of 0.8 K/W connects that interface to a cold reservoir. Overall temperature difference is 20 K.

  1. Parallel equivalent: Rp=(1/2+1/3)−1=1.2 K/W.
  2. Total resistance Rt=1.2+0.8=2; total rate Q˙=10 W.
  3. Drop across shared film is 8 K; drop across either branch is 12 K.
  4. Branch rates are 6 W and 4 W, which sum to 10 W. They are not individually 10 W.
