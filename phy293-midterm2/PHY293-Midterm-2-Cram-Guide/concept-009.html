Photoelectric effect and stopping potential

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Use one photon’s energy to pay the work function and the electron’s kinetic energy.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · The work function is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal. In the single-photon model, increasing frequency changes energy per photon; increasing intensity at fixed frequency mainly changes the number of absorption events.

Assumptions

Emission requires hf≥ϕ. Ignore multiphoton effects. Use a positive stopping-potential magnitude Vs.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Energy conservation gives Kmax=hf−ϕ=hc/λ−ϕ=eVs. Threshold means Kmax=0, so λmax=hc/ϕ. A retarding detector bias V requires enough energy to escape and cross the bias: hc/λ≥ϕ+eV.

Example

Level 3 · For the PS6 cesium value ϕ=2.1eV, threshold wavelength is about 590 nm. With a 2.0 V retarding bias, the detection cutoff becomes 1240/(2.1+2.0)≃302nm. Emission and successful collection are different conditions.
Common trap: A negative computed kinetic energy means no emission, not an electron with negative kinetic energy.

PROBLEM TYPE

2. Atomic transition → photoelectric emission

Level 1 · The work function is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal. In the single-photon model, increasing frequency changes energy per photon; increasing intensity at fixed frequency mainly changes the number of absorption events.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Energy conservation gives Kmax=hf−ϕ=hc/λ−ϕ=eVs. Threshold means Kmax=0, so λmax=hc/ϕ. A retarding detector bias V requires enough energy to escape and cross the bias: hc/λ≥ϕ+eV.

Worked example

2025 Midterm I(a–b): He+ emits in the n=2→1 transition; the metal stopping potential is 34.0 V. Find wavelength and work function.

  1. Eγ=13.6(2)2(1−1/4)=40.8eV.
  2. λ=1240/40.8≃30.4nm.
  3. Kmax=eVs=34.0eV, so ϕ=40.8−34.0=6.8eV. The stopping potential describes the electron, not the photon.
