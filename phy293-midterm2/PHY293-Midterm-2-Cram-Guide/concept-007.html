Photon energy and momentum

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

A photon transfers discrete energy and momentum.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Light interferes like a wave but exchanges energy in packets. A photon of frequency f has energy hf; its momentum is along its direction of travel.

Assumptions

Photon relations apply to massless light in vacuum. Use separate momentum relations for massive electrons.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Eγ=hf=ℏω=hc/λ,pγ=Eγ/c=h/λ. Shortening wavelength increases both energy and momentum. Conserving photon number is not generally the same as conserving energy.

Example

Level 3 · Halving a photon wavelength doubles its energy and momentum. For 620 nm light, Eγ=2.00eV. Two such photons have 4 eV total, but the ordinary single-photon photoelectric model treats each absorption event separately.

PROBLEM TYPE

1. Photon counts and thermal radiation

Level 1 · Light interferes like a wave but exchanges energy in packets. A photon of frequency f has energy hf; its momentum is along its direction of travel.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Eγ=hf=ℏω=hc/λ,pγ=Eγ/c=h/λ. Shortening wavelength increases both energy and momentum. Conserving photon number is not generally the same as conserving energy.

Worked example

Author-created example: a source emits 1.00 mW at 620 nm isotropically. How many photons per second cross a 1.00 cm² detector at 1.00 m?

  1. Each photon has E=1240/620=2.00eV=3.204×10−19J.
  2. The source rate is 10−3/(3.204×10−19)=3.12×1015s−1.
  3. The detector fraction is 10−4/(4π)=7.96×10−6. The detected rate is about 2.48×1010s−1, before efficiency losses.

PROBLEM TYPE

3. Compton wavelength, recoil energy and direction

Level 1 · Light interferes like a wave but exchanges energy in packets. A photon of frequency f has energy hf; its momentum is along its direction of travel.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Eγ=hf=ℏω=hc/λ,pγ=Eγ/c=h/λ. Shortening wavelength increases both energy and momentum. Conserving photon number is not generally the same as conserving energy.

Worked example

Author-created example: a 0.100 nm photon scatters through 90 degrees from an electron initially at rest. Estimate the outgoing wavelength and electron kinetic energy.

  1. At 90 degrees, Δλ=λC≃0.002426nm. Thus λf≃0.102426nm.
  2. Ei=1240/0.100=12400eV, and Ef=1240/0.102426≃12106eV.
  3. The electron gains about 294 eV. If the scattered photon goes upward, the recoil electron has a downward momentum component: conservation fixes the sign.
