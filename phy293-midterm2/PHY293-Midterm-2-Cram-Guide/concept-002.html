Complex amplitudes and phase

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Foundations

Why this matters

Square the magnitude of an amplitude, not the complex number itself.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A quantum wavefunction can have real and imaginary parts. Its phase describes how it interferes with other amplitudes; it is not itself a probability.

Assumptions

For z=a+ib, the conjugate is z*=a−ib. A global phase multiplies the entire state by the same unit complex number.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · |z|2=z*z=a2+b2,eiθ=cosθ+isinθ. Thus |eiθ|=1. But |z1+z2|2=|z1|2+|z2|2+2Re(z1*z2): relative phase changes the cross term.

Example

Level 3 · For z=(3+4i)/5, |z|2=1. Equal contributions with phases 0 and π cancel: 1+eiπ=0. Multiplying both contributions by i does not change any probability.
Common trap: Do not replace a coherent sum of amplitudes by a sum of probabilities.

PROBLEM TYPE

10. Time evolution and moving probability density

Level 1 · A quantum wavefunction can have real and imaginary parts. Its phase describes how it interferes with other amplitudes; it is not itself a probability.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · |z|2=z*z=a2+b2,eiθ=cosθ+isinθ. Thus |eiθ|=1. But |z1+z2|2=|z1|2+|z2|2+2Re(z1*z2): relative phase changes the cross term.

Worked example

For Ψ(x,0)=(ψ1+ψ2)/2, obtain the probability density and its oscillation period.

  1. Ψ=(ψ1e−iE1t/ℏ+ψ2e−i4E1t/ℏ)/2.
  2. |Ψ|2=(ψ12+ψ22)/2+ψ1ψ2cos(3E1t/ℏ).
  3. The period is T=2πℏ/(3E1). Integrating over the entire box removes the cross term and leaves total probability one. The energy probabilities remain 1/2 each throughout.
