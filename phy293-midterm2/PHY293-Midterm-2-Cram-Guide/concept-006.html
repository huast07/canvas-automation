Variance and standard deviation

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Foundations

Why this matters

Quantify uncertainty using the distribution, not the size of its mean.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · The standard deviation is the root-mean-square distance from the mean. It measures spread across identically prepared measurements.

Assumptions

The relevant moments must exist. For momentum in a box, use energy and symmetry as in PS8 rather than introducing an unneeded operator method.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Expand (q−⟨q⟩)2, take its average, and use normalization: σq2=⟨q2⟩−⟨q⟩2. For a mean of zero, σq=⟨q2⟩, which need not vanish.

Example

Level 3 · A variable taking values 1 and 3 with equal probabilities has mean 2, second moment 5, variance 1 and standard deviation 1. The second moment is 5, not 4.
Common trap: A zero mean momentum is compatible with nonzero kinetic energy.

PROBLEM TYPE

6. Uncertainty, linewidth and confinement estimates

Level 1 · The standard deviation is the root-mean-square distance from the mean. It measures spread across identically prepared measurements.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Expand (q−⟨q⟩)2, take its average, and use normalization: σq2=⟨q2⟩−⟨q⟩2. For a mean of zero, σq=⟨q2⟩, which need not vanish.

Worked example

PS8 Q3: a state has lifetime 1 ms and emits around 550 nm. Estimate energy spread and fractional wavelength spread.

  1. Using the course estimate ΔE≃ℏ/(2τ), obtain ΔE≃3.29×10−13eV.
  2. The photon energy is 1240/550≃2.25eV.
  3. For a narrow line, Δλ/λ≃ΔE/E≃1.46×10−13. This is a linewidth estimate, not an exact oscillation period.
