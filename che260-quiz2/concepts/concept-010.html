Thermal resistance and its units

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Express each heat-transfer law as temperature difference divided by resistance.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Thermal resistance measures temperature difference needed per watt of heat transfer: R=ΔT/Q˙, with units K/W.

Assumptions

Use a law valid for the chosen geometry and conditions. A resistance based on radiation can depend on temperature. Area-normalized resistance has different units.

Reasoning / derivation

Rearrange each law into Q˙=ΔT/R: Rwall=LkA,Rconv=1hA,Rrad=1hradA. The heat-flux version uses R′′ in m²·K/W and q′′=ΔT/R′′. For a uniform plane area, R′′=RA.

Example

Original example: a wall with L=0.1, k=0.5, A=2 has R=0.1 K/W. A 20 K surface difference drives 200 W.
