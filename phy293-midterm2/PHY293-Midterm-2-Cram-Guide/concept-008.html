Blackbody radiation and the classical limit

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Understand why quantized energy fixes the short-wavelength failure of classical theory.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A blackbody absorbs incident light ideally and has an equilibrium radiation spectrum determined by temperature. Increasing temperature increases total emission and shifts the wavelength peak shorter.

Assumptions

The course formula is spectral energy density per wavelength. Do not interchange it with spectral density per frequency or emitted power without the appropriate conversion.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · ρλ=8πhcλ51ehc/(λkBT)−1. For x=hc/(λkBT)≪1, ex−1≃x, giving ρλ≃8πkBT/λ4. At short wavelength the exponential suppresses the ultraviolet divergence. At fixed area, total emitted power scales as T4, and λmaxT is constant.

Example

Level 3 · Doubling absolute temperature multiplies total radiated power by 16 and halves the peak wavelength. The Taylor-limit calculation is PS6 Q3 background, explicitly not a standalone tested mathematical exercise; the physical quantization idea belongs to Lecture 2-2.
Common trap: A limit valid at long wavelength is not valid throughout the spectrum.

Worked example

Author-created example: a source emits 1.00 mW at 620 nm isotropically. How many photons per second cross a 1.00 cm² detector at 1.00 m?

  1. Each photon has E=1240/620=2.00eV=3.204×10−19J.
  2. The source rate is 10−3/(3.204×10−19)=3.12×1015s−1.
  3. The detector fraction is 10−4/(4π)=7.96×10−6. The detected rate is about 2.48×1010s−1, before efficiency losses.
