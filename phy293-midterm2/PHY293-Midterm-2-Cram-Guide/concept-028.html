Infinite square well: eigenfunctions and energies

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Quantum dynamics

Why this matters

Derive quantization from two boundary conditions and normalization.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A particle confined to 0<x<L with U=0 inside and infinite walls outside has zero probability outside and zero wavefunction at both endpoints.

Assumptions

This formula uses L as the full width. A well from −a to a has width 2a; substituting a as the width gives a factor-of-four energy error.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Inside, ψ′′+k2ψ=0, so ψ=Asinkx+Bcoskx. At x=0, B=0. At x=L, nontrivial states require kL=nπ. Normalization uses ∫0Lsin2(nπx/L)dx=L/2. Hence ψn=2/Lsin(nπx/L),En=n2π2ℏ22mL2,n=1,2,….

Example

Level 3 · For an electron and L=1 nm, E1≃0.376eV, E2≃1.504eV, and E3≃3.384eV. There are n−1 interior nodes. n=0 would give the zero function, which cannot normalize.
Common trap: The allowed energies scale as n², not n; the ground-state energy is not zero.

Worked example

For the ground state on [0,L], calculate the probability asked in PS8 Q5(b), from −L/4 to 3L/4.

  1. Normalize the sine: ψ=2/Lsin(πx/L) inside, zero outside.
  2. The requested interval overlaps the well on [0,3L/4]. Use sin2u=(1−cos2u)/2.
  3. P=[xL−sin(2πx/L)2π]03L/4=34+12π≃0.909.
  4. The posted solution instead uses [L/4,3L/4] and obtains about 0.818. Both integrations are correct for their respective limits; the printed limits differ.
