Schrödinger equation and the Hamiltonian

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Quantum dynamics

Why this matters

Use the quantum evolution law rather than the classical wave equation for matter waves.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A free nonrelativistic particle has E=p2/(2m). Combined with E=ℏω and p=ℏk, this gives ω=ℏk2/(2m), unlike light’s linear relation ω=ck.

Assumptions

The Schrödinger equation is a quantum postulate motivated by these relations, not a theorem derived from classical mechanics. Assume one spatial dimension, a nonrelativistic particle, and real scalar potential U.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · For Ψ=ei(kx−ωt), ∂x2Ψ=−k2Ψ and ∂tΨ=−iωΨ. Therefore the operators reproduce the free-particle energy relation. Adding potential energy gives iℏ∂tΨ=H^Ψ,H^=−ℏ22md2dx2+U(x).

Example

Level 3 · If U=0, substitution of a plane-wave component requires ℏω=ℏ2k2/(2m). Doubling k quadruples kinetic energy. A localized free state is a packet of these components.
Common trap: The kinetic term contains h-bar squared; an apparent missing square in extracted slides is not a new convention.

11. Inverse Schrödinger problems

Worked example

Author-created variant of 2024 III(b): ψ=Ae−αx2, α>0. Find U and E if U(0)=0.

  1. ψ′=−2αxψ, and ψ′′=(4α2x2−2α)ψ.
  2. U=E−ℏ2α/m+2ℏ2α2x2/m.
  3. At x=0, the specified reference gives E=ℏ2α/m. Thus U=2ℏ2α2x2/m.
  4. Without U(0)=0 or another energy reference, both U and E can shift by the same constant. Normalization would separately give A=(2α/π)1/4.
