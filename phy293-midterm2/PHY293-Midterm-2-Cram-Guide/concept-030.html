Orthonormality and projection coefficients

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Superposition

Why this matters

Use orthogonal energy eigenfunctions as a coordinate system for wavefunctions.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Normalized eigenfunctions behave like perpendicular unit vectors, with an integral replacing the dot product. Any suitable state in the well can be expanded in this basis.

Assumptions

Use the complex-conjugate inner product ∫ψm*ψndx. Coefficient-probability shortcuts require an orthonormal basis, not arbitrary unnormalized sines.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · If ∫ψm*ψndx=δmn and Ψ=∑cnψn, multiplying by ψm* and integrating gives cm=∫ψm*Ψdx,∫|Ψ|2dx=∑|cn|2. Cross terms vanish in this full-domain norm, not at every position.

Example

Level 3 · For Ψ=A(2ψ1+iψ2), normalization gives 5|A|2=1, so a real positive A is 1/5. If the question instead supplies A[2sin(πx/L)+sin(3πx/L)], the norm is 5A2L/2, giving A=2/(5L).
Common trap: Do not confuse a coefficient in front of a raw sine with a coefficient in front of a normalized eigenfunction.

PROBLEM TYPE

9. Superposition normalization and energy moments

Level 1 · Normalized eigenfunctions behave like perpendicular unit vectors, with an integral replacing the dot product. Any suitable state in the well can be expanded in this basis.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · If ∫ψm*ψndx=δmn and Ψ=∑cnψn, multiplying by ψm* and integrating gives cm=∫ψm*Ψdx,∫|Ψ|2dx=∑|cn|2. Cross terms vanish in this full-domain norm, not at every position.

Worked example

2025 QII: Ψ=A(ψ2+12ψ3), where the basis states are normalized. Find A, energy moments and energy uncertainty.

  1. 1=A2(1+1/4), so A=2/5. Probabilities are 4/5 and 1/5.
  2. E2=4E1, E3=9E1. Thus ⟨E⟩=(4/5)4E1+(1/5)9E1=5E1.
  3. ⟨E2⟩=(4/5)16E12+(1/5)81E12=29E12, so σE=29−25E1=2E1.
  4. The course uncertainty estimate gives Δt~ℏ/(4E1). The actual density beat period is 2πℏ/(5E1), a different quantity.
