Area changes between interfaces

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Use the actual area for each contact, solid region and exposed surface.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

A heat source can contact a small patch of a larger plate. Its contact resistance uses the small patch; convection uses the exposed surface.

Assumptions

The lecture example approximates conduction spreading through an assigned plate region. A detailed spreading-resistance solution is beyond this simple model.

Reasoning / derivation

Keep areas labelled: Rtotal=1hcAc+LkAplate+1hcombinedAexposed. For N identical independent regions, calculate per-region power then multiply by N. Do not use the total plate area for each small contact.

Example

Original example: hc=1000, Ac=0.001, h=10, As=0.1 give contact and convection resistances both 1 K/W. Neglect plate resistance; a 40 K allowable rise gives 20 W per source.

PROBLEM TYPE

7 · Contact-limited electronic cooling

A heat source can contact a small patch of a larger plate. Its contact resistance uses the small patch; convection uses the exposed surface.

Reasoning / derivation

Keep areas labelled: Rtotal=1hcAc+LkAplate+1hcombinedAexposed. For N identical independent regions, calculate per-region power then multiply by N. Do not use the total plate area for each small contact.

Worked example

Original example: source contact area 0.001 m², contact conductance 1000 W/(m² K), exposed plate area 0.1 m², h=10 W/(m² K). Neglect plate resistance and radiation. Air is 20°C; source limit is 60°C.

  1. Rc=1/(1000×0.001)=1 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×0.1)=1 K/W.
  3. Pmax=(60−20)/(1+1)=20 W.
  4. Plate temperature is 20+20(1)=40 ∘C; contact jump is 20(1)=20 K, reaching the 60°C source limit.
