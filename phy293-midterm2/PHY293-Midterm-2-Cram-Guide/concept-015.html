Franck–Hertz excitation thresholds

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Relate current dips to discrete atomic excitation energies.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · An accelerated electron can collide elastically with an atom without exciting it, or lose a discrete energy in an inelastic excitation. Current decreases when electrons lose enough energy that they cannot reach the collector.

Assumptions

Use the problem’s stated initial atomic state. Do not assume ground-state hydrogen when the measured spacing is incompatible with its first excitation.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · An electron accelerated through a voltage interval ΔV gains energy eΔV. Regular dip spacing estimates ΔE=eΔV. The subsequently emitted photon obeys hc/λ=ΔE. Repeated dips can result from repeated inelastic collisions.

Example

Level 3 · The 2023 paper gives 1.9 V spacing in hydrogen. E3−E2=13.6(1/4−1/9)≃1.89eV, consistent with 2→3 excitation and 3→2 emission, about 656 nm. It is not the 10.2 eV ground-to-first-excited transition.
