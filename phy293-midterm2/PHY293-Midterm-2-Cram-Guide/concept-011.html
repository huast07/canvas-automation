Compton scattering and recoil

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Use wavelength shift for the photon and energy/momentum conservation for the electron.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A photon scattering from an initially stationary electron can give it kinetic energy. The outgoing photon has lower energy and therefore longer wavelength.

Assumptions

The Compton formula assumes a free electron initially at rest; atomic binding is neglected. The photon scattering angle is not the electron recoil angle.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · λf−λi=hmec(1−cosθ),Ke=hc(1λi−1λf). This follows from energy and vector momentum conservation with relativistic electron energy. Components give pex=h/λi−(h/λf)cosθ and pey=−(h/λf)sinθ. Use their signs to determine recoil direction.

Example

Level 3 · For PS6’s 0.0711 nm photon at 180 degrees, Δλ≃0.00486nm, λf≃0.07596nm, and recoil energy is about 1.11 keV. At zero angle, the shift and recoil energy vanish.
Common trap: Compute recoil energy from the difference of reciprocal wavelengths, not hc divided by the wavelength shift.

PROBLEM TYPE

3. Compton wavelength, recoil energy and direction

Level 1 · A photon scattering from an initially stationary electron can give it kinetic energy. The outgoing photon has lower energy and therefore longer wavelength.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · λf−λi=hmec(1−cosθ),Ke=hc(1λi−1λf). This follows from energy and vector momentum conservation with relativistic electron energy. Components give pex=h/λi−(h/λf)cosθ and pey=−(h/λf)sinθ. Use their signs to determine recoil direction.

Worked example

Author-created example: a 0.100 nm photon scatters through 90 degrees from an electron initially at rest. Estimate the outgoing wavelength and electron kinetic energy.

  1. At 90 degrees, Δλ=λC≃0.002426nm. Thus λf≃0.102426nm.
  2. Ei=1240/0.100=12400eV, and Ef=1240/0.102426≃12106eV.
  3. The electron gains about 294 eV. If the scattered photon goes upward, the recoil electron has a downward momentum component: conservation fixes the sign.
