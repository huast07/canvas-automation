Recovering a potential from a wavefunction

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Quantum dynamics

Why this matters

Differentiate the supplied state and use the Schrödinger equation to infer the potential.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Sometimes the state is given and the potential is the unknown. The equation can be rearranged rather than solved as a differential equation.

Assumptions

For a stationary state, U=E+(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′/ψ where ψ is nonzero. At nodes return to the undivided equation or take a valid limit. Without a specified energy reference, E and U are determined only up to a common additive constant.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · For Ψ=ψe−iωt, E=ℏω. For ψ=Ae−αx2, ψ′′/ψ=4α2x2−2α, giving U(x)=E−ℏ2αm+2ℏ2α2mx2. Choosing U(0)=0 gives E=ℏ2α/m.

Example

Level 3 · For PS8’s sine with time factor e−iωt, the interior potential is U=ℏω−ℏ2π2/(2ma2). The sine must also be normalized before computing probabilities. The infinite walls are a confinement assumption, not something obtained by division at the zeros.
Common trap: An unspecified additive constant in the potential cannot be determined from the spatial wavefunction alone.

PROBLEM TYPE

11. Inverse Schrödinger problems

Level 1 · Sometimes the state is given and the potential is the unknown. The equation can be rearranged rather than solved as a differential equation.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · For Ψ=ψe−iωt, E=ℏω. For ψ=Ae−αx2, ψ′′/ψ=4α2x2−2α, giving U(x)=E−ℏ2αm+2ℏ2α2mx2. Choosing U(0)=0 gives E=ℏ2α/m.

Worked example

Author-created variant of 2024 III(b): ψ=Ae−αx2, α>0. Find U and E if U(0)=0.

  1. ψ′=−2αxψ, and ψ′′=(4α2x2−2α)ψ.
  2. U=E−ℏ2α/m+2ℏ2α2x2/m.
  3. At x=0, the specified reference gives E=ℏ2α/m. Thus U=2ℏ2α2x2/m.
  4. Without U(0)=0 or another energy reference, both U and E can shift by the same constant. Normalization would separately give A=(2α/π)1/4.
