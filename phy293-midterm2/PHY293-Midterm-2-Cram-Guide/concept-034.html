Constant-potential regions: allowed and forbidden

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Boundaries

Why this matters

Choose oscillatory, exponential, or linear solutions by the sign of E−U.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Inside a region of constant potential, the sign of kinetic-energy parameter E−U determines the curvature equation. Quantum states can have nonzero amplitude in a region where E<U.

Assumptions

Assume a constant mass and a constant finite potential in each region. Infinite walls are handled separately. At E=U the wavenumber is zero and the limiting solution needs care.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · From ψ′′=2m(U−E)ψ/ℏ2: if E>U, ψ=Aeikx+Be−ikx with k=2m(E−U)/ℏ; if E<U, ψ=Ceκx+De−κx with κ=2m(U−E)/ℏ; if E=U, ψ=A+Bx. For bound-state tails, remove terms that diverge at infinity.

Example

Level 3 · In the 2025 well with exterior potentials 0.4 and 0.2 eV, an energy between 0 and 0.2 eV gives decay on both sides and oscillation in the U=0 interior. Between 0.2 and 0.4 eV, the right side becomes oscillatory. Above 0.4 eV, all three regions are oscillatory.
Common trap: E<U does not mean ψ is zero. A semi-infinite forbidden region has an evanescent tail, not a transmitted travelling wave.

PROBLEM TYPE

12. Piecewise potential and boundary matching

Level 1 · Inside a region of constant potential, the sign of kinetic-energy parameter E−U determines the curvature equation. Quantum states can have nonzero amplitude in a region where E<U.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · From ψ′′=2m(U−E)ψ/ℏ2: if E>U, ψ=Aeikx+Be−ikx with k=2m(E−U)/ℏ; if E<U, ψ=Ceκx+De−κx with κ=2m(U−E)/ℏ; if E=U, ψ=A+Bx. For bound-state tails, remove terms that diverge at infinity.

Worked example

2025 QIII route: write the bound-state forms for the asymmetric 0.4 eV / 0 / 0.2 eV well of width L=10 nm, using constant effective mass m*=0.067me and 0<E<0.2 eV.

  1. Left: AeκLx; interior: Bcoskx+Csinkx; right: De−κR(x−L).
  2. k=2m*E/ℏ, κL=2m*(0.4eV−E)/ℏ, and κR=2m*(0.2eV−E)/ℏ. Convert eV to joules if inserting SI constants.
  3. At 0: A=B, κLA=kC. At L: BcoskL+CsinkL=D and −kBsinkL+kCcoskL=−κRD.
  4. These are the requested matching equations. This historical extension is included for method practice; confirm its precise scope against forthcoming Lecture 2-11.
