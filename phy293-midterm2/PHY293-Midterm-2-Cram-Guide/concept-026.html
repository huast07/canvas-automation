Separation of variables and stationary states

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Quantum dynamics

Why this matters

Separate spatial shape from the time phase for an energy eigenstate.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · When the potential is time-independent, try a product Ψ(x,t)=ψ(x)T(t). A stationary state has a definite energy and a probability density that does not change with time.

Assumptions

Time-independent U is required for this standard separation. A general superposition is not one product with one energy unless all occupied components have the same energy.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Substitute and divide locally by ψT: iℏT−1dTdt=−ℏ22mψ−1d2ψdx2+U=E. The time-only and space-only expressions must equal a constant. Thus T=e−iEt/ℏ, and −ℏ2ψ′′/(2m)+Uψ=Eψ. Since the phase has unit magnitude, |Ψ|2=|ψ|2.

Example

Level 3 · An eigenstate with E=E1 changes its complex phase in time but keeps the same position density. Multiplying an arbitrary initial superposition by one phase generally fails because its components have different energies.
Common trap: Stationary does not mean the complex wavefunction is independent of time.
