Convection and the coefficient h

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Surface heat exchange

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Relate surface-to-fluid heat flow to area, airflow and the convection coefficient.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Convection uses the solid surface temperature Ts and bulk fluid temperature T∞: Q˙=hAs(Ts−T∞). The coefficient h combines flow, geometry and fluid-property effects.

Assumptions

Use the exposed surface area. Forced convection uses externally driven flow; natural convection uses buoyancy. A fan usually changes h; adding area does not by itself specify a new h.

Reasoning / derivation

Define h=Q˙/[As(Ts−T∞)]. Its units are W/(m²·K). Rearrangement gives Ts=T∞+Q˙/(hAs), which makes surface cooling at fixed power explicit.

Example

Original example: 60 W leaves a 0.2 m² surface into 20°C air with h=10. Then Ts=20+60/(10×0.2)=50 ∘C.
