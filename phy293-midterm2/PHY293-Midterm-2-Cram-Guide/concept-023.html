Uncertainty estimate of atomic size

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Wave packets

Why this matters

Balance confinement kinetic energy against Coulomb attraction.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Compressing an electron lowers Coulomb potential energy but raises its typical kinetic energy through uncertainty. A minimum in their sum gives a characteristic atomic size.

Assumptions

This is a scale estimate using p~ℏ/r, not an exact derivation from the standard-deviation inequality. Treat the proton as fixed and use nonrelativistic kinetic energy.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · E(r)~ℏ22mr2−kee2r. Differentiate: dE/dr=−ℏ2/(mr3)+kee2/r2. Setting it to zero gives r~ℏ2/(mkee2)=a0. The curvature at that point is positive.

Example

Level 3 · As r approaches zero, the positive 1/r² term eventually dominates the negative 1/r term, preventing collapse in this estimate. The exact agreement with the Bohr radius reflects the chosen order-one factors, as the PS8 solution itself notes.
Common trap: A scale argument does not establish the exact ground-state wavefunction.

6. Uncertainty, linewidth and confinement estimates

Level 1 · Compressing an electron lowers Coulomb potential energy but raises its typical kinetic energy through uncertainty. A minimum in their sum gives a characteristic atomic size.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · E(r)~ℏ22mr2−kee2r. Differentiate: dE/dr=−ℏ2/(mr3)+kee2/r2. Setting it to zero gives r~ℏ2/(mkee2)=a0. The curvature at that point is positive.

Worked example

PS8 Q3: a state has lifetime 1 ms and emits around 550 nm. Estimate energy spread and fractional wavelength spread.

  1. Using the course estimate ΔE≃ℏ/(2τ), obtain ΔE≃3.29×10−13eV.
  2. The photon energy is 1240/550≃2.25eV.
  3. For a narrow line, Δλ/λ≃ΔE/E≃1.46×10−13. This is a linewidth estimate, not an exact oscillation period.
