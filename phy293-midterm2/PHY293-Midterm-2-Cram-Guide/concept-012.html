Bohr model and quantized orbits

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Derive the allowed radii and energies from force balance and angular-momentum quantization.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · The Bohr model postulates stationary circular orbits for one bound charged particle around a nucleus. The orbit does not radiate continuously; radiation accompanies transitions.

Assumptions

Use a nonrelativistic one-particle Coulomb model with nuclear charge Ze and fixed nucleus unless reduced mass is requested. Write the Coulomb constant as ke to distinguish it from wavenumber k.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Combine mv2/r=keZe2/r2 with mvr=nℏ. Substitution yields rn=n2ℏ2mkeZe2,En=K+U=−m(keZe2)22ℏ2n2. Since force balance gives K=keZe2/(2r) and U=−keZe2/r, total energy is negative. For an electron, En=−13.6Z2/n2eV.

Example

Level 3 · In He+ (Z=2), E1=−54.4eV and E2=−13.6eV. Doubling Z at fixed n halves the orbital radius but quadruples the binding-energy magnitude.
Common trap: The potential energy alone is twice the total bound-state energy; do not use it as the level energy.
