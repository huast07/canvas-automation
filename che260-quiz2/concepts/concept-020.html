Sensible heating and phase change

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Energy applications

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Connect heat delivery to temperature change, stream cooling or phase change.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Sensible heating changes temperature; latent heating changes phase. Heat transfer determines the energy-delivery rate, which can then be linked to temperature change, evaporation or melting time.

Assumptions

Use appropriate heat capacity or latent heat and consistent units. A pure substance changing phase at fixed pressure can remain at its phase-change temperature; do not apply mcΔT to the latent step.

Reasoning / derivation

Q=mcpΔT,Q=mℓ,m˙evap=Q˙/ℓ,tmelt=mℓ/Q˙. For a flowing stream with no work and negligible kinetic/potential changes, Q˙=m˙cp(Tout−Tin), with m˙=ρuAflow. Flow area is a cross-section, unlike the lateral area used for radial heat transfer.

Example

Original example: 20 W enters 1 kg of ice at 0°C; using ℓ=334000 J/kg, melting takes 16700 s=4.639 h. A stream with m˙=0.5 kg/s, cp=2000 J/(kgK), losing 1000 W cools by 1 K.

5 · Sphere, heat leakage and phase change

Sensible heating changes temperature; latent heating changes phase. Heat transfer determines the energy-delivery rate, which can then be linked to temperature change, evaporation or melting time.

Reasoning / derivation

Q=mcpΔT,Q=mℓ,m˙evap=Q˙/ℓ,tmelt=mℓ/Q˙. For a flowing stream with no work and negligible kinetic/potential changes, Q˙=m˙cp(Tout−Tin), with m˙=ρuAflow. Flow area is a cross-section, unlike the lateral area used for radial heat transfer.

Worked example

Original example: ice at 0°C fixes an inner surface at 0°C. A spherical insulation shell has radii 0.1 and 0.2 m, k=0.05 W/(m K). Outside air is 20°C with h=10 W/(m² K). Ignore radiation. Find heat gain and time to melt 1 kg of ice, with latent heat 334 kJ/kg.

  1. Rs=(1/0.1−1/0.2)/(4π×0.05)=7.95775 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×4π×0.22)=0.198944 K/W.
  3. Q˙=20/(7.95775+0.198944)=2.45197 W.
  4. t=334000/2.45197=136217 s=37.838 h. Temperature stays at the melting point while ice remains under the model assumptions.
