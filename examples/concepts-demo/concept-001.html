Rate of change

Illustrative course: Differential equations · Foundations

Why this matters

The rate at which a quantity changes.

Scope: unknown

From first principles

If y(t) represents a quantity, its average rate of change over a short interval h is [y(t + h) − y(t)] / h. The derivative is the limit of that average as h approaches zero, when the limit exists.

Assumptions

Assume the functions and operations used below are defined under the stated conditions. Check the cited source for the full hypotheses.

Reasoning / derivation

For y(t) = t², [y(t + h) − y(t)] / h = [(t + h)² − t²] / h = 2t + h. As h approaches zero, the rate approaches 2t.

Example

At t = 3, y′(3) = 6. Over a very short interval, the value initially increases at about 6 units per unit of time.
