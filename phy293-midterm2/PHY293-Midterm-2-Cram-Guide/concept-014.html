Mass scaling, reduced mass and muonic atoms

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Light and atoms

Why this matters

Know when the fixed-nucleus approximation changes and how mass rescales spectra.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A real nucleus also moves. The relative-coordinate problem uses the reduced mass μ=mM/(m+M). If M is much larger than m, then μ≃m.

Assumptions

Use the approximation explicitly requested. PS7 Q1 assumes infinite proton mass; replacing it with reduced mass would answer a different approximation. Heavy muonic atoms eventually challenge the point-nucleus/nonrelativistic model.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · In Bohr formulas replace m with μ: rn∝1/μ and |En|∝μ. Relative to an electron in fixed-nucleus hydrogen, rn=a0meμn2Z,En=−13.6μmeZ2n2eV.

Example

Level 3 · With the PS7 approximation mμ=207me, the muonic-hydrogen ground radius is a0/207≃2.56×10−13m, and E1≃−2.82keV. Its 2→1 photon has wavelength about 0.587 nm.
Common trap: Changing mass affects both radius and energy, in opposite directions.

PROBLEM TYPE

2. Atomic transition → photoelectric emission

Level 1 · A real nucleus also moves. The relative-coordinate problem uses the reduced mass μ=mM/(m+M). If M is much larger than m, then μ≃m.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · In Bohr formulas replace m with μ: rn∝1/μ and |En|∝μ. Relative to an electron in fixed-nucleus hydrogen, rn=a0meμn2Z,En=−13.6μmeZ2n2eV.

Worked example

2025 Midterm I(a–b): He+ emits in the n=2→1 transition; the metal stopping potential is 34.0 V. Find wavelength and work function.

  1. Eγ=13.6(2)2(1−1/4)=40.8eV.
  2. λ=1240/40.8≃30.4nm.
  3. Kmax=eVs=34.0eV, so ϕ=40.8−34.0=6.8eV. The stopping potential describes the electron, not the photon.
