Electrical power and fixed-input problems

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Energy applications

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Recognize when power sets the heat rate and resistance sets temperature.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

A steady device with a specified electrical input must reject that input as heat if there is no other output or storage. Better cooling changes the required device temperature.

Assumptions

Specified fixed power; all input becomes heat; steady operation. If voltage is fixed and resistance varies with temperature, power need not remain fixed.

Reasoning / derivation

Electrical input is P=VI=I2Relec=V2/Relec. Balance gives Q˙out=P. With a linear thermal network, Tdevice=T∞+PRthermal. Fixed temperatures instead give Q˙=ΔT/R; distinguish the two boundary conditions.

Example

Original example: a 10 W chip in 25°C air with thermal resistance 4 K/W reaches 65°C. Reducing resistance to 2 K/W gives 45°C, while the rejected heat remains 10 W.

PROBLEM TYPE

1 · Predict changes before calculating

A steady device with a specified electrical input must reject that input as heat if there is no other output or storage. Better cooling changes the required device temperature.

Reasoning / derivation

Electrical input is P=VI=I2Relec=V2/Relec. Balance gives Q˙out=P. With a linear thermal network, Tdevice=T∞+PRthermal. Fixed temperatures instead give Q˙=ΔT/R; distinguish the two boundary conditions.

Worked example

Original example: a 10 W chip is connected through a wall to 25°C air. Total resistance falls from 4 to 2 K/W. What changes?

  1. All electrical input exits as heat at steady state: Q˙=10 W in both cases.
  2. Initial temperature is 25+10(4)=65 ∘C.
  3. After improved cooling it is 25+10(2)=45 ∘C. At fixed chip temperature instead, the heat rate would rise when resistance falls.

PROBLEM TYPE

7 · Contact-limited electronic cooling

A steady device with a specified electrical input must reject that input as heat if there is no other output or storage. Better cooling changes the required device temperature.

Reasoning / derivation

Electrical input is P=VI=I2Relec=V2/Relec. Balance gives Q˙out=P. With a linear thermal network, Tdevice=T∞+PRthermal. Fixed temperatures instead give Q˙=ΔT/R; distinguish the two boundary conditions.

Worked example

Original example: source contact area 0.001 m², contact conductance 1000 W/(m² K), exposed plate area 0.1 m², h=10 W/(m² K). Neglect plate resistance and radiation. Air is 20°C; source limit is 60°C.

  1. Rc=1/(1000×0.001)=1 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×0.1)=1 K/W.
  3. Pmax=(60−20)/(1+1)=20 W.
  4. Plate temperature is 20+20(1)=40 ∘C; contact jump is 20(1)=20 K, reaching the 60°C source limit.
