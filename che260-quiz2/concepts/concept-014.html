Contact resistance and conductance

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Model temperature jumps at imperfect solid-to-solid contacts.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Real contacting solids touch at microscopic spots, leaving gaps. A finite interface temperature jump is therefore possible even when the interface is treated as geometrically thin.

Assumptions

Use contact area, which may differ from the wall or convection area. The double-prime contact resistance is area-specific.

Reasoning / derivation

Define hc=Q˙/[Ac(T1−T2)]. Then Rc=1hcAc=Rc′′Ac,Rc′′=1hc. Increasing contact pressure, smoother contact or suitable conductive filler generally increases conductance; verify material-specific data.

Example

Original example: Rc′′=0.002 m2K/W, Ac=0.01 m2, and 50 W give Rc=0.2 K/W and a 10 K temperature jump.

PROBLEM TYPE

2 · Composite wall and surface temperatures

Real contacting solids touch at microscopic spots, leaving gaps. A finite interface temperature jump is therefore possible even when the interface is treated as geometrically thin.

Reasoning / derivation

Define hc=Q˙/[Ac(T1−T2)]. Then Rc=1hcAc=Rc′′Ac,Rc′′=1hc. Increasing contact pressure, smoother contact or suitable conductive filler generally increases conductance; verify material-specific data.

Worked example

Original example: 20°C indoor air and 0°C outdoor air; area 2 m²; wall thickness 0.1 m, k=0.5 W/(m K); inside h=10 and outside h=20 W/(m² K). Ignore radiation and contact resistance.

  1. Ri=1/(10×2)=0.05, Rw=0.1/(0.5×2)=0.1, Ro=1/(20×2)=0.025 K/W.
  2. Rtotal=0.175 K/W; Q˙=20/0.175=114.286 W.
  3. Ts,i=20−114.286(0.05)=14.286 ∘C.
  4. Ts,o=0+114.286(0.025)=2.857 ∘C. The drop across the wall is 11.429 K, which reproduces the same heat rate by Fourier law.

PROBLEM TYPE

7 · Contact-limited electronic cooling

Real contacting solids touch at microscopic spots, leaving gaps. A finite interface temperature jump is therefore possible even when the interface is treated as geometrically thin.

Reasoning / derivation

Define hc=Q˙/[Ac(T1−T2)]. Then Rc=1hcAc=Rc′′Ac,Rc′′=1hc. Increasing contact pressure, smoother contact or suitable conductive filler generally increases conductance; verify material-specific data.

Worked example

Original example: source contact area 0.001 m², contact conductance 1000 W/(m² K), exposed plate area 0.1 m², h=10 W/(m² K). Neglect plate resistance and radiation. Air is 20°C; source limit is 60°C.

  1. Rc=1/(1000×0.001)=1 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×0.1)=1 K/W.
  3. Pmax=(60−20)/(1+1)=20 W.
  4. Plate temperature is 20+20(1)=40 ∘C; contact jump is 20(1)=20 K, reaching the 60°C source limit.
