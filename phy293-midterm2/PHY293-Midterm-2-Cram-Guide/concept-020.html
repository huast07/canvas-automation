Fourier series and symmetry

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Wave packets

Why this matters

Extract amplitudes of orthogonal sinusoidal modes.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A periodic shape can be assembled from a constant part plus sine and cosine harmonics. The coefficients tell you how strongly each spatial frequency contributes.

Assumptions

For period P use kn=2πn/P and integrate over any complete period. Keep the Fourier convention consistent; half-interval sine expansions use different prefactors.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · f=A02+∑n≥1(Ancosknx+Bnsinknx). Multiply by a basis function and integrate: orthogonality isolates its coefficient, e.g. An=(2/P)∫fcosknxdx. For even f on a symmetric interval, every sine coefficient vanishes because the integrand is odd.

Example

Level 3 · For the even unit pulse of width a in PS8 Q1, A0/2=a/P, An=2sin(nπa/P)/(nπ), and Bn=0. At duty cycle a/P=1/2, all even n coefficients vanish and A1=2/π.
Common trap: The constant term is A0/2, not A0.

PROBLEM TYPE

7. Fourier coefficients and packet width

Level 1 · A periodic shape can be assembled from a constant part plus sine and cosine harmonics. The coefficients tell you how strongly each spatial frequency contributes.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · f=A02+∑n≥1(Ancosknx+Bnsinknx). Multiply by a basis function and integrate: orthogonality isolates its coefficient, e.g. An=(2/P)∫fcosknxdx. For even f on a symmetric interval, every sine coefficient vanishes because the integrand is odd.

Worked example

PS8 Q1: find coefficients for a centered unit pulse of width a repeated every P.

  1. Even symmetry gives Bn=0. Average value gives A0/2=a/P.
  2. An=(2/P)∫−a/2a/2cos(2πnx/P)dx.
  3. Integration yields An=2sin(nπa/P)/(nπ). Harmonics vanish when n a/P is an integer. A narrow pulse generally requires more high-frequency components.
