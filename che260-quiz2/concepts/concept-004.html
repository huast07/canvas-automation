Fourier law and its sign

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Conduction

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Use the temperature gradient to determine conductive heat flow and its direction.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Fourier law relates heat flowing in a chosen direction to temperature change along that direction: Q˙x=−kAdT/dx.

Assumptions

Local isotropic conduction; the 1D expression assumes temperature varies principally along one coordinate.

Reasoning / derivation

If temperature falls as x increases, dT/dx<0. The minus sign then makes Q˙x>0: heat travels toward increasing x, from hot to cold. For steady conduction through a slab with constant k and A and no generation, integrate: Q˙L=−kA(T2−T1).

Example

Original example: k=0.5, A=2 m2, L=0.1 m, surfaces 40°C and 20°C. Heat rate from the 40°C face to the 20°C face is 200 W.
