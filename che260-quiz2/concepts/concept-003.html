Thermal conductivity and material trends

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Foundations

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Relate material conductivity to temperature gradients and property trends.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Thermal conductivity k, in W/(m·K), measures how readily a material carries heat for a specified temperature gradient. It is a material property; convection coefficient h describes a surface–flow situation.

Assumptions

Use properties at a representative temperature when the constant-property approximation is reasonable. Trends are not universal rankings of all solids, liquids and gases.

Reasoning / derivation

From q′′=−kdT/dx, fixed flux implies |dT/dx|=|q′′|/k. Metals generally conduct much better than gases and insulation; alloys often conduct less well than pure constituents. Temperature dependence differs among materials. Interpolation: k(T)=ka+(kb−ka)(T−Ta)/(Tb−Ta).

Example

Original example: at q′′=1000 W/m2, materials with k=400 and k=0.04 have gradient magnitudes 2.5 and 25,000 K/m. Large conductivity means a small gradient at the same flux.

Worked example

Original example: a 10 W chip is connected through a wall to 25°C air. Total resistance falls from 4 to 2 K/W. What changes?

  1. All electrical input exits as heat at steady state: Q˙=10 W in both cases.
  2. Initial temperature is 25+10(4)=65 ∘C.
  3. After improved cooling it is 25+10(2)=45 ∘C. At fixed chip temperature instead, the heat rate would rise when resistance falls.
