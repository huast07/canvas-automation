de Broglie wavelength: supplied relativistic relation

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Matter waves

Why this matters

Handle the relativistic formula already supplied in the problem set without adding later relativity lectures.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · The relation λ=h/p still holds at high speed; the nonrelativistic relation between momentum and energy fails.

Assumptions

PS7 Q4 calls E the total energy. If a different question gives kinetic energy K, first set E=K+mc2. This limited application is sourced in PS7; later Lorentz-transform topics remain outside the cutoff.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · From E2=(pc)2+(mc2)2, λ=hcE2−(mc2)2. For kinetic energy use pc=K(K+2mc2). When K≪mc2, this reduces to the nonrelativistic result.

Example

Level 3 · For an electron with total E=2.00 MeV and rest energy 0.511 MeV, pc≃1.934MeV, so λ≃6.41×10−13m.
Common trap: Adding rest energy when E already includes it double-counts the rest energy.

PROBLEM TYPE

4. Matter-wave wavelength → diffraction angle

Level 1 · The relation λ=h/p still holds at high speed; the nonrelativistic relation between momentum and energy fails.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · From E2=(pc)2+(mc2)2, λ=hcE2−(mc2)2. For kinetic energy use pc=K(K+2mc2). When K≪mc2, this reduces to the nonrelativistic result.

Worked example

PS7 Q5 route: a thermal neutron at 300 K is diffracted by planes 2.82 Å apart. Find the first-order detector angle.

  1. K=3kBT/2≃6.21×10−21J.
  2. λ=h/2mnK≃1.45A˚.
  3. Bragg gives sinθ=1.45/(2×2.82), hence θ≈14.9 degrees. The detector angle asked for is 2θ≈29.8 degrees.
