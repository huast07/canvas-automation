Time evolution and interference in superpositions

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Superposition

Why this matters

Give every energy component its own time phase and retain interference in position space.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Different energy eigenstates rotate in phase at different rates. Their relative phase changes, so the position density of a coherent superposition can oscillate even while energy probabilities stay fixed.

Assumptions

Assume time-independent U and no measurement/decoherence during evolution. The explicit cosine formula below assumes real coefficients and real eigenfunctions.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Ψ(x,t)=∑ncnψn(x)e−iEnt/ℏ. For two real components, |Ψ|2=ca2ψa2+cb2ψb2+2cacbψaψbcos[(Eb−Ea)t/ℏ]. The interference integrates to zero over the full domain by orthogonality, but generally survives pointwise or in weighted integrals such as mean position.

Example

Level 3 · For (ψ1+ψ2)/2, the density cross term is ψ1ψ2cos(3E1t/ℏ). Its period is 2πℏ/(3E1). Energy probabilities remain 1/2 each. A single eigenstate has no relative phase to produce this interference.
Common trap: Orthogonality removes integrated cross terms; it does not set the product of two different eigenfunctions to zero at every x.

PROBLEM TYPE

10. Time evolution and moving probability density

Level 1 · Different energy eigenstates rotate in phase at different rates. Their relative phase changes, so the position density of a coherent superposition can oscillate even while energy probabilities stay fixed.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Ψ(x,t)=∑ncnψn(x)e−iEnt/ℏ. For two real components, |Ψ|2=ca2ψa2+cb2ψb2+2cacbψaψbcos[(Eb−Ea)t/ℏ]. The interference integrates to zero over the full domain by orthogonality, but generally survives pointwise or in weighted integrals such as mean position.

Worked example

For Ψ(x,0)=(ψ1+ψ2)/2, obtain the probability density and its oscillation period.

  1. Ψ=(ψ1e−iE1t/ℏ+ψ2e−i4E1t/ℏ)/2.
  2. |Ψ|2=(ψ12+ψ22)/2+ψ1ψ2cos(3E1t/ℏ).
  3. The period is T=2πℏ/(3E1). Integrating over the entire box removes the cross term and leaves total probability one. The energy probabilities remain 1/2 each throughout.
