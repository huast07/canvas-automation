Plane-wall temperature profiles

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Conduction

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Explain straight profiles, changing slopes and continuity across ideal contacts.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Temperature profiles show where the temperature changes most strongly and which material offers more resistance.

Assumptions

A straight line within a plane layer requires steady 1D conduction, constant area and conductivity, and no internal heat generation. Different layers may have different slopes.

Reasoning / derivation

Energy conservation makes Q˙ constant through an unheated layer. Fourier law gives dT/dx=−Q˙/(kA), a constant, so T(x)=T1+(T2−T1)x/L. With perfect contact, temperature is continuous across an interface; slope can change when k changes.

Example

Original example: for q′′=100 W/m2, slopes in materials with k=1 and k=0.1 are −100 and −1000 K/m. Across 0.01 m of each, drops are 1 K and 10 K.
