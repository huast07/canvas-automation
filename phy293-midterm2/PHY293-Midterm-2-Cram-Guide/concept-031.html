Energy measurements, moments and collapse

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Superposition

Why this matters

Separate possible outcomes, their probabilities, and the expectation value.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · For a normalized state expanded in energy eigenfunctions, an ideal energy measurement returns one of the occupied eigenvalues. The average over many preparations is their weighted mean.

Assumptions

Assume the box’s nondegenerate energy levels and ideal projective measurement. A repeat measurement of the same observable without an intervening change returns the same result.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · P(En)=|cn|2,⟨E⟩=∑|cn|2En,⟨E2⟩=∑|cn|2En2. Then σE=⟨E2⟩−⟨E⟩2. After measuring En, the state becomes the corresponding eigenstate up to phase.

Example

Level 3 · For (2ψ1+ψ2)/5, probabilities are 4/5 and 1/5. Since E2=4E1, ⟨E⟩=8E1/5, ⟨E2⟩=4E12, and σE=6E1/5. A measured energy of 8E1/5 is not possible even though it is the mean.
Common trap: A negative or imaginary coefficient does not give a negative or imaginary probability.

PROBLEM TYPE

9. Superposition normalization and energy moments

Level 1 · For a normalized state expanded in energy eigenfunctions, an ideal energy measurement returns one of the occupied eigenvalues. The average over many preparations is their weighted mean.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · P(En)=|cn|2,⟨E⟩=∑|cn|2En,⟨E2⟩=∑|cn|2En2. Then σE=⟨E2⟩−⟨E⟩2. After measuring En, the state becomes the corresponding eigenstate up to phase.

Worked example

2025 QII: Ψ=A(ψ2+12ψ3), where the basis states are normalized. Find A, energy moments and energy uncertainty.

  1. 1=A2(1+1/4), so A=2/5. Probabilities are 4/5 and 1/5.
  2. E2=4E1, E3=9E1. Thus ⟨E⟩=(4/5)4E1+(1/5)9E1=5E1.
  3. ⟨E2⟩=(4/5)16E12+(1/5)81E12=29E12, so σE=29−25E1=2E1.
  4. The course uncertainty estimate gives Δt~ℏ/(4E1). The actual density beat period is 2πℏ/(5E1), a different quantity.
