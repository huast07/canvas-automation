Diffraction: gratings versus Bragg planes

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Matter waves

Why this matters

Choose the correct path-difference geometry and angle convention.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Constructive interference occurs when the path difference between two contributions is an integer multiple of wavelength. Transmission gratings and reflection from crystal planes have different path differences.

Assumptions

Normal-incidence grating: dsinθ. Bragg reflection with θ measured from the planes: 2dsinθ. A detector angle may be 2θ.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · mλ=dsinθ(grating),nλ=2dsinθ(Bragg). For matter-wave diffraction, obtain λ from momentum first. Check |sinθ|≤1; otherwise that order cannot occur.

Example

Level 3 · PS7 thermal neutrons at 300 K have about 0.0388 eV and wavelength 1.45 Å. For d=2.82 Å, first-order Bragg reflection gives θ≈14.9 degrees and detector angle 2θ≈29.8 degrees.
Common trap: The factor of two is geometric, not a correction to the de Broglie wavelength.

PROBLEM TYPE

4. Matter-wave wavelength → diffraction angle

Level 1 · Constructive interference occurs when the path difference between two contributions is an integer multiple of wavelength. Transmission gratings and reflection from crystal planes have different path differences.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · mλ=dsinθ(grating),nλ=2dsinθ(Bragg). For matter-wave diffraction, obtain λ from momentum first. Check |sinθ|≤1; otherwise that order cannot occur.

Worked example

PS7 Q5 route: a thermal neutron at 300 K is diffracted by planes 2.82 Å apart. Find the first-order detector angle.

  1. K=3kBT/2≃6.21×10−21J.
  2. λ=h/2mnK≃1.45A˚.
  3. Bragg gives sinθ=1.45/(2×2.82), hence θ≈14.9 degrees. The detector angle asked for is 2θ≈29.8 degrees.
