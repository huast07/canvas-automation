Series resistances

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Resistance networks

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Add resistances along a path carrying the same heat rate.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

In series, the same heat rate passes through successive layers and boundaries. Temperature drops add.

Assumptions

Steady state, no internal source or side loss along the series path. Every resistance must use its own correct area.

Reasoning / derivation

Each drop is ΔTi=Q˙Ri. Summing gives Thot−Tcold=Q˙∑iRi,Rtotal=∑iRi. When endpoints are air temperatures, include convection boundaries. When endpoints are specified surface temperatures, do not add imaginary convection resistances beyond them.

Example

Original example: hi=10, ho=20, A=2, L=0.1, k=0.5, air at 20°C and 0°C. Resistances are 0.05, 0.10 and 0.025 K/W. Total 0.175 gives 114.286 W.

PROBLEM TYPE

2 · Composite wall and surface temperatures

In series, the same heat rate passes through successive layers and boundaries. Temperature drops add.

Reasoning / derivation

Each drop is ΔTi=Q˙Ri. Summing gives Thot−Tcold=Q˙∑iRi,Rtotal=∑iRi. When endpoints are air temperatures, include convection boundaries. When endpoints are specified surface temperatures, do not add imaginary convection resistances beyond them.

Worked example

Original example: 20°C indoor air and 0°C outdoor air; area 2 m²; wall thickness 0.1 m, k=0.5 W/(m K); inside h=10 and outside h=20 W/(m² K). Ignore radiation and contact resistance.

  1. Ri=1/(10×2)=0.05, Rw=0.1/(0.5×2)=0.1, Ro=1/(20×2)=0.025 K/W.
  2. Rtotal=0.175 K/W; Q˙=20/0.175=114.286 W.
  3. Ts,i=20−114.286(0.05)=14.286 ∘C.
  4. Ts,o=0+114.286(0.025)=2.857 ∘C. The drop across the wall is 11.429 K, which reproduces the same heat rate by Fourier law.

PROBLEM TYPE

3 · Parallel and mixed networks

In series, the same heat rate passes through successive layers and boundaries. Temperature drops add.

Reasoning / derivation

Each drop is ΔTi=Q˙Ri. Summing gives Thot−Tcold=Q˙∑iRi,Rtotal=∑iRi. When endpoints are air temperatures, include convection boundaries. When endpoints are specified surface temperatures, do not add imaginary convection resistances beyond them.

Worked example

Original example: two paths of 2 and 3 K/W connect a hot node to an interface. A shared outer film of 0.8 K/W connects that interface to a cold reservoir. Overall temperature difference is 20 K.

  1. Parallel equivalent: Rp=(1/2+1/3)−1=1.2 K/W.
  2. Total resistance Rt=1.2+0.8=2; total rate Q˙=10 W.
  3. Drop across shared film is 8 K; drop across either branch is 12 K.
  4. Branch rates are 6 W and 4 W, which sum to 10 W. They are not individually 10 W.
