Steady energy balance

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Foundations

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Use conservation to distinguish balanced heat flow from energy accumulation.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Steady state means no energy accumulation with time. It does not require uniform temperature or zero heat flow.

Assumptions

Define the system boundary and account for all relevant heat, work and mass-energy transfers. For a solid with electrical input, count electrical power once.

Reasoning / derivation

For a stationary solid, Q˙in+Pelec−Q˙out=dUdt. At steady state the right side is zero. For an unheated layer with no storage, Q˙in=Q˙out. With thermal generation in a solid, use q˙genV instead of double-counting its energy source.

Example

Week 7 Q2: imposed flux is 20 W/m², but convection removes 20(50−30)=400 W/m2. Net storage is −380 W/m2: the wall cools. It is not steady.

PROBLEM TYPE

1 · Predict changes before calculating

Steady state means no energy accumulation with time. It does not require uniform temperature or zero heat flow.

Reasoning / derivation

For a stationary solid, Q˙in+Pelec−Q˙out=dUdt. At steady state the right side is zero. For an unheated layer with no storage, Q˙in=Q˙out. With thermal generation in a solid, use q˙genV instead of double-counting its energy source.

Worked example

Original example: a 10 W chip is connected through a wall to 25°C air. Total resistance falls from 4 to 2 K/W. What changes?

  1. All electrical input exits as heat at steady state: Q˙=10 W in both cases.
  2. Initial temperature is 25+10(4)=65 ∘C.
  3. After improved cooling it is 25+10(2)=45 ∘C. At fixed chip temperature instead, the heat rate would rise when resistance falls.

PROBLEM TYPE

6 · Surface balance with radiation

Steady state means no energy accumulation with time. It does not require uniform temperature or zero heat flow.

Reasoning / derivation

For a stationary solid, Q˙in+Pelec−Q˙out=dUdt. At steady state the right side is zero. For an unheated layer with no storage, Q˙in=Q˙out. With thermal generation in a solid, use q˙genV instead of double-counting its energy source.

Worked example

Original example: a 1 m² surface at 350 K with emissivity 0.8 loses heat to 300 K air and walls. h=10 W/(m² K). What electrical power maintains it?

  1. Q˙conv=10(1)(350−300)=500 W.
  2. Q˙rad=0.8(5.67×10−8)(1)(3504−3004)=313.268 W.
  3. Energy balance gives P=813.268 W. Alternatively hrad=313.268/50=6.26535, so combined h is 16.26535 W/(m² K).
  4. For a prescribed power and unknown Ts, solve P=hA(Ts−T∞)+εσA(Ts4−Tsurr4); do not evaluate h_rad at an inconsistent guessed temperature.
