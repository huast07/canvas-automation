QUESTION CATEGORY 01

Estimating a solution with Euler’s method

Identify the current point, calculate its slope, and take one or more numerical steps.

Slope at a point

For y′ = f(t, y), f(t, y) gives the instantaneous slope at that point.

Reasoning / derivation. The derivative is the limiting rate of change. Substituting the current t and y into f gives the local rate specified by the equation.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Step size and approximation

The step size h is the change in t used for each estimate. The resulting estimate need not equal the exact solution.

Reasoning / derivation. Using slope × horizontal change to approximate vertical change gives y(next) ≈ y(current) + h × slope.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Worked example

For y′=t+y, y(0)=1, estimate y(1) using two Euler steps of h=0.5.

  1. At t=0 and y=1, the slope is 1, so y1=1+0.5(1)=1.5.
  2. At t=0.5 and y1=1.5, the slope is 2, so y2=1.5+0.5(2)=2.5.
  3. After two steps t=1, giving y(1)≈2.5; this is not the exact value.

Practice progression

Start with one concept. First practise calculating f(t, y) at a given point and identifying a step size.

Combine concepts. Then combine slope evaluation, repeated steps and an interpretation of numerical error.

QUESTION CATEGORY 02

Solve a first-order linear IVP

Recognize a linear form and use the product rule.

Integrating factors

An integrating factor converts the linear left-hand side to a product derivative.

Reasoning / derivation. Require μ′=pμ so that (μy)′=μ(y′+py).

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Worked example

Solve y′+y=0 with y(0)=1.

  1. Choose μ=et.
  2. Then (ety)′=0, so ety=C.
  3. Use y(0)=1 to obtain y=e−t, and check by substitution.

Practice progression

Start with one concept. First recognize a linear equation and apply the product rule.

Combine concepts. Then combine the factor, integration and initial condition.

QUESTION CATEGORY 03

Compare numerical methods

Compute and compare one-step estimates.

Improved Euler method

Use the average of the starting and predicted ending slopes.

Reasoning / derivation. The predictor uses k₁, and the correction averages k₁ and k₂.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Worked example

For y′=t+y, y(0)=1 and h=0.5, compute one improved Euler step.

  1. k₁=1 and the Euler predictor is 1.5.
  2. k₂=0.5+1.5=2.
  3. y₁=1+0.5(1+2)/2=1.75.

Practice progression

Start with one concept. First compute an individual slope.

Combine concepts. Then combine a predictor, a second slope and an error interpretation.

QUESTION CATEGORY 04

Evaluate a Laplace transform

Use the definition and check convergence.

Laplace transform

The transform is an improper integral whose convergence depends on s.

Reasoning / derivation. For e^(at), direct integration gives 1/(s−a) when s>a.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Worked example

Find the transform of e^(2t).

  1. Combine exponentials in the defining integral.
  2. Integrate e^(−(s−2)t) over [0,∞).
  3. The result is 1/(s−2) for s>2.

Practice progression

Start with one concept. First practise exponent laws and improper integrals.

Combine concepts. Then combine convergence, transform properties and an IVP.

Illustrative lecture 1: foundations

Rate of change

If y(t) represents a quantity, its average rate of change over a short interval h is [y(t + h) − y(t)] / h. The derivative is the limit of that average as h approaches zero, when the limit exists.

Reasoning / derivation. For y(t) = t², [y(t + h) − y(t)] / h = [(t + h)² − t²] / h = 2t + h. As h approaches zero, the rate approaches 2t.

Example. At t = 3, y′(3) = 6. Over a very short interval, the value initially increases at about 6 units per unit of time.

Illustrative lecture 4: numerical methods

Euler’s method

A differential equation y′=f(t,y) specifies the slope at each point. Starting from a known value, use that slope to estimate the value a small time step later.

Reasoning / derivation. For a short step h, the definition of derivative suggests y(t+h)−y(t)h≈f(t,y(t)). Rearranging gives y(t+h)≈y(t)+hf(t,y(t)), an approximation in general.

Example. If y′=t+y and y(0)=1, one step of h=0.5 gives y(0.5)≈1+0.5(0+1)=1.5.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Improved Euler method

Improved Euler estimates the slope at both the beginning and a predicted end point, then averages them.

Reasoning / derivation. Let k1=f(tn,yn), predict y*=yn+hk1, and take k2=f(tn+h,y*). Averaging slopes gives yn+1=yn+h2(k1+k2).

Example. For y′=t+y, y(0)=1, and h=0.5: k1=1, y*=1.5, k2=2, so the improved estimate is 1.75.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Runge–Kutta method

A four-stage Runge–Kutta method combines several slope estimates across a step.

Reasoning / derivation. The classical method uses slopes k1,k2,k3,k4 with weights 1,2,2,1: yn+1=yn+h6(k1+2k2+2k3+k4). Its error-order proof requires additional assumptions and a Taylor-series argument.

Example. If y′=1, every stage slope is 1, so the method advances y by exactly h.

Illustrative lecture 2: first-order equations

Separation of variables

A separable equation has the form y′=g(t)h(y); its factors can be treated separately where h(y)≠0.

Reasoning / derivation. Since y′=dy/dt, division and integration give ∫dyh(y)=∫g(t)dt+C on an interval where division is valid. Check excluded constant solutions separately.

Example. For y′=2y and y(0)=1, separation gives ln|y|=2t+C; the initial condition selects y=e2t.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Integrating factors

A linear first-order equation has the form y′+p(t)y=q(t). An integrating factor makes its left side a product derivative.

Reasoning / derivation. The product rule gives (μy)′=μy′+μ′y. Matching μ(y′+py) requires μ′=pμ; one choice is μ=exp(∫p(t)dt).

Example. For y′+y=0, choose μ=et. Thus (ety)′=0, and y(0)=1 gives y=e−t.

Sources
  • Illustrative material for this demonstration; not a course lecture.

Illustrative lecture 5: transforms

Laplace transform

The Laplace transform expresses a function of time as an integral depending on s, when that integral converges.

Reasoning / derivation. For f(t)=eat, the definition gives ∫0∞e−steatdt=1s−a,s>a.

Example. With a=2, the transform is 1/(s−2), for s>2.
