Quantifying a negligible resistance

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Design problems

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Estimate the error caused by omitting a small thermal resistance.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

A high-conductivity layer may have negligible resistance, but justify this by its share of total resistance rather than by the material name alone.

Assumptions

Compare the same network with and without the proposed term. Small local temperature drop does not automatically make all heat-transfer modes negligible.

Reasoning / derivation

If Rtotal=Rrest+Rs, omitting Rs increases fixed-ΔT predicted heat rate. Relative error compared with the full result is Q˙omit−Q˙fullQ˙full=RsRrest. The layer temperature drop is Q˙Rs.

Example

Original example: Rs=0.001, Rrest=1 K/W. Omitting the small layer overpredicts heat rate by 0.1%. At 20 W its temperature drop is only 0.02 K.

PROBLEM TYPE

4 · Insulated cylindrical pipe

A high-conductivity layer may have negligible resistance, but justify this by its share of total resistance rather than by the material name alone.

Reasoning / derivation

If Rtotal=Rrest+Rs, omitting Rs increases fixed-ΔT predicted heat rate. Relative error compared with the full result is Q˙omit−Q˙fullQ˙full=RsRrest. The layer temperature drop is Q˙Rs.

Worked example

Original example: inner pipe surface at 70°C, insulation from radius 0.01 to 0.02 m with k=0.05 W/(m K), outer h=10 W/(m² K), air 20°C. Use 1 m length; no inner film or pipe resistance is needed because inner surface temperature is given.

  1. Rins=ln(2)/(2π×0.05×1)=2.20636 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×2π×0.02×1)=0.795775 K/W.
  3. Q˙=(70−20)/(2.20636+0.795775)=16.6548 W, or 16.6548 W/m.
  4. Ts,o=20+16.6548(0.795775)=33.2534 ∘C. End temperatures bound the result.
