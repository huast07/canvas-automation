Potential step: matching and reflection

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Boundaries

Why this matters

Use continuity to connect incident, reflected and transmitted amplitudes.

Scope: inferred

From first principles

Level 1 · At a finite step a wave must fit smoothly across the interface. Even above the step, this matching can require a reflected component.

Assumptions

For the worked extension take constant mass, incidence from the left, U=0 on the left and U=U0 on the right, with E>U0 and no incoming wave from the right. Reflection/transmission probability formulas are derived here from matching and flux; Lecture 2-10 explicitly supplies the matching method, but the next lecture’s exact scope is still unavailable.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · Write ψI=Aeik1x+Be−ik1x and ψII=Ceik2x. At x=0: A+B=C and k1(A−B)=k2C. Hence r=B/A=(k1−k2)/(k1+k2), t=C/A=2k1/(k1+k2). Travelling-wave flux is proportional to k times amplitude squared, so R=|r|2,T=(k2/k1)|t|2,R+T=1.

Example

Level 3 · If k2=k1/2, then r=1/3 and t=4/3, while R=1/9 and T=8/9. A transmitted amplitude greater than one does not violate probability conservation because its speed is smaller. For E<U0 on a semi-infinite right side, the physical tail decays and net transmitted travelling flux is zero.
Common trap: Amplitude transmission squared alone is not T when the two wavenumbers differ.
