Units and energy bookkeeping

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Foundations

Why this matters

Keep photon energy, kinetic energy and potential energy distinct.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · A joule and an electronvolt measure the same quantity: energy. One electron gains 1 eV of kinetic energy when accelerated through a potential difference of magnitude 1 V. Frequency is cycles per second; angular frequency counts radians per second.

Assumptions

Use e=1.602×10−19C, 1eV=1.602×10−19J, and ℏ=h/(2π).

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · From c=fλ and E=hf, obtain E=hc/λ. In convenient units, hc≃1240eVnm. If a kinetic-energy equation uses a mass in kg, convert the energy to joules before taking a square root.

Example

Level 3 · A 400 nm photon has E=1240/400=3.10eV. An electron with 3.10 eV kinetic energy has v=2(3.10)(1.602×10−19)/me≃1.04×106m/s. The photon itself moves at c, not at this electron speed.
Common trap: An electric potential in volts is not an energy until multiplied by charge; h and h-bar are not interchangeable.
