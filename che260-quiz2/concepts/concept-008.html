Radiation, emissivity and absorption

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Surface heat exchange

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Distinguish emission, absorption and net radiation; use absolute temperature.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

A blackbody emits σT4 per unit area. A real surface emits εσT4, where emissivity 0≤ε≤1. Absorptivity describes the fraction of incident radiation absorbed.

Assumptions

Temperatures in fourth powers must be kelvin. The simple net-exchange formula applies to a small surface in a large isothermal enclosure; parallel black plates also use the blackbody difference. General two-body exchange requires more geometry and surface information.

Reasoning / derivation

For the enclosure model, subtract absorbed surroundings radiation from emission: Q˙rad=εσA(Ts4−Tsurr4). Under irradiation G, absorbed power is αGA; an opaque surface has reflected fraction 1−α. Solar absorptivity and thermal infrared emissivity need not be equal. Visible white does not imply low infrared emissivity; Lecture 20 lists white paint near 0.90.

Example

Original example: A=1 m2, ε=0.8, Ts=350 K, Tsurr=300 K give Q˙rad=313.268 W, using σ=5.67×10−8.

PROBLEM TYPE

6 · Surface balance with radiation

A blackbody emits σT4 per unit area. A real surface emits εσT4, where emissivity 0≤ε≤1. Absorptivity describes the fraction of incident radiation absorbed.

Reasoning / derivation

For the enclosure model, subtract absorbed surroundings radiation from emission: Q˙rad=εσA(Ts4−Tsurr4). Under irradiation G, absorbed power is αGA; an opaque surface has reflected fraction 1−α. Solar absorptivity and thermal infrared emissivity need not be equal. Visible white does not imply low infrared emissivity; Lecture 20 lists white paint near 0.90.

Worked example

Original example: a 1 m² surface at 350 K with emissivity 0.8 loses heat to 300 K air and walls. h=10 W/(m² K). What electrical power maintains it?

  1. Q˙conv=10(1)(350−300)=500 W.
  2. Q˙rad=0.8(5.67×10−8)(1)(3504−3004)=313.268 W.
  3. Energy balance gives P=813.268 W. Alternatively hrad=313.268/50=6.26535, so combined h is 16.26535 W/(m² K).
  4. For a prescribed power and unknown Ts, solve P=hA(Ts−T∞)+εσA(Ts4−Tsurr4); do not evaluate h_rad at an inconsistent guessed temperature.
