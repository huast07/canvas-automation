Critical insulation radius

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Design problems

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Explain why added cylindrical insulation can initially reduce total resistance.

Scope: inferred

From first principles

Adding cylindrical insulation both increases conduction resistance and increases exposed area, reducing convection resistance. The two effects compete.

Assumptions

Optional extension evidenced by the review tutorial, not explicitly named in the confirmed sections. Constant k and h; cylindrical radial model; no added radiation or contact changes.

Reasoning / derivation

Ignoring inner terms independent of outer radius, R(r)=ln(r/ri)2πkL+12πrhL. Differentiate: dR/dr=1/(2πkLr)−1/(2πhLr2). Resistance is minimum at rcrit=k/h. Below it, added insulation reduces total resistance; above it, resistance rises. At fixed temperature difference heat rate is maximum there; at fixed power wire temperature is minimum there.

Example

Review Q3: k=0.15, h=24, so rcrit=0.00625 m=6.25 mm. Initial outer radius is 2.1 mm; doubling 1 mm insulation gives 3.1 mm, still below critical. Interface temperature decreases. The printed 6.25 mm is a critical radius, not the requested interface temperature.

8 · Insulation design and performance checks

Adding cylindrical insulation both increases conduction resistance and increases exposed area, reducing convection resistance. The two effects compete.

Reasoning / derivation

Ignoring inner terms independent of outer radius, R(r)=ln(r/ri)2πkL+12πrhL. Differentiate: dR/dr=1/(2πkLr)−1/(2πhLr2). Resistance is minimum at rcrit=k/h. Worked example

Current review Problem 3: wire diameter 2.2 mm, length 14 m, 1 mm plastic insulation, k=0.15 W/(m K), current 13 A, voltage drop 8 V, ambient 30°C, h=24 W/(m² K). Find interface temperature and effect of doubling insulation thickness.

  1. P=VI=104 W, ri=0.0011 m, ro=0.0021 m.
  2. R=ln(ro/ri)/(2πkL)+1/(2πroLh). Then Ti=30+104R. The independently calculated result is given in the verification note below. Interface temperature: 58.555°C initially and 54.057°C after doubling.
  3. Doubling insulation thickness makes ro=0.0031 m, not 0.0042 m. Both outer radii are below k/h=0.00625 m.
  4. Increasing radius toward the critical radius reduces total resistance and thus lowers wire temperature at fixed 104 W. The printed 6.25 mm answer is a radius, not a temperature.
