Transitions, spectral lines and ionization

Example. Level 3 · For an electron and L=1 nm, E1≃0.376eV, E2≃1.504eV, and E3≃3.384eV. There are n−1 interior nodes. n=0 would give the zero function, which cannot normalize.
Common trap: The allowed energies scale as n², not n; the ground-state energy is not zero.

Before you finish a problem

  1. Identify whether the number supplied is energy, kinetic energy, voltage, amplitude or probability.
  2. Normalize before using probabilities; distinguish a normalized eigenfunction from a raw sine.
  3. Use h for E=hf and λ=h/p, and h-bar for E=ℏω, p=ℏk and Schrödinger evolution.
  4. Convert eV to joules before combining with SI mass; keep nm, Å and fm distinct.
  5. For emission, subtract lower level from higher level; ground state is n=1.
  6. For each potential region, compare E with that region’s U before selecting the solution form.
  7. At finite steps match ψ and ψ′; at an infinite wall impose ψ=0.
  8. Do not erase interference pointwise just because eigenfunctions are orthogonal.
  9. Compute ⟨E²⟩ separately from ⟨E⟩². A mean need not be an allowed measurement.
  10. Use the actual interval printed in a question. PS8 Q5(b) and its solution use different intervals.
  11. Separate exact σxσp≥ℏ/2 from order-of-magnitude confinement estimates.
  12. Check that probabilities sum to one, decay goes toward the correct infinity, and answers have the requested units.
