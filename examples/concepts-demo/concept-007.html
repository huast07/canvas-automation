Integrating factors

Illustrative course: Differential equations · First-order equations

Why this matters

Transforms a first-order linear equation into a product derivative.

Scope: unknown

From first principles

A linear first-order equation has the form y′+p(t)y=q(t). An integrating factor makes its left side a product derivative.

Assumptions

Assume the functions and operations used below are defined under the stated conditions. Check the cited source for the full hypotheses.

Reasoning / derivation

The product rule gives (μy)′=μy′+μ′y. Matching μ(y′+py) requires μ′=pμ; one choice is μ=exp(∫p(t)dt).

Example

For y′+y=0, choose μ=et. Thus (ety)′=0, and y(0)=1 gives y=e−t.

PROBLEM TYPE

Solve a first-order linear IVP

An integrating factor converts the linear left-hand side to a product derivative.

Reasoning / derivation

Require μ′=pμ so that (μy)′=μ(y′+py).

Worked example

Solve y′+y=0 with y(0)=1.

  1. Choose μ=et.
  2. Then (ety)′=0, so ety=C.
  3. Use y(0)=1 to obtain y=e−t, and check by substitution.
