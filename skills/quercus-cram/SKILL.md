---
name: quercus-cram
description: Guide a time-limited Quercus exam preparation session with triage, practice and feedback.
---
Ask for the course, exam time and minutes available, and locate the Quercus project. Before creating a cram plan, concepts page, diagnostic or practice, inspect existing study data and run `python3 study.py check-ready COURSE_ID`. If course lecture sync is missing, invoke and carry out `quercus-lecture-notes` yourself. Next, if the saved scope is missing, stale, incomplete or for a different exam, invoke and carry out `quercus-exam-scope` yourself and obtain the student's review. Reuse a completed sync and a current, reviewed scope when they already exist. If either prerequisite cannot be completed, explain the blocker and pause rather than starting cram with an unverified scope.

After both prerequisites and before initiating the concepts page or tutoring, offer the student a chance to upload or provide local paths to extra content, such as textbooks, filled-in lecture notes, exam guidance or past papers. Wait for the materials or an explicit decision to continue without them; silence is not a decision. Do not repeat the offer if the student has already been offered the opportunity and responded, or clearly said there is nothing more to add. A request for a required missing file alone does not replace the opportunity to add other materials. Invoke `quercus-document-import` yourself for provided files (`lecture` for filled notes, `scope` for guidance, `past-exam` for past papers, `other` for textbooks), index them, and update or re-check the scope if the new material could change it. Report unreadable files and source gaps.

Use `python3 study_plan.py cram COURSE_ID --exam ISO_TIME_WITH_OFFSET --minutes N` to calculate a feasible time budget, then use the documented scope and problem types to propose a time-boxed editable plan, prioritizing confirmed topics and essential prerequisites. State omissions, stale or missing sources and uncertain priorities. Start with a concise conceptual refresher and use quercus-exam-prep to guide progressively harder representative questions with hints and corrective feedback. Finish with a mixed mock and feedback if time permits; in a very short session use one or two representative questions and feedback instead of a full mock. Reserve time to review errors. Do not promise complete coverage or a grade.

Before tutoring or the diagnostic, use the concepts-page workflow in `quercus-exam-prep`: inspect the reviewed scope, lecture sources and question categories; build and open the local HTML page with a categorized visual mind map of all identified concepts and their labeled relationships, explanatory graphics, problem-type concepts, first-principles lecture explainers, derivations, examples, citations and explicit gaps. Inventory every available lecture/source before mapping: capture individual concepts, distinct methods and their variants and conditions, relationships, and relevant problem types. Populate the lecture-by-lecture `source_coverage` checklist, including missing or partially reviewed materials, and label confirmed versus inferred or out-of-scope concepts. Adapt the taxonomy to the subject rather than assuming a STEM course. Tailor tutoring priority and depth to the available time without collapsing methods into vague headings or omitting known concepts silently. For each lecture, cover every substantive broad concept separately and generate linked first-principles concept subpages as described there. Start the readiness listener and wait for the student to select “I understand” or explicitly say they are ready in chat before any diagnostic or practice. Then progressively practise individual prerequisites, combined concepts and a representative midterm-level question for each evidenced category, adapting depth and ordering to the time available, before the short mock. If sources or the saved scope are unavailable, report the blocker rather than inventing content; if browser opening fails, provide the saved local page path.
