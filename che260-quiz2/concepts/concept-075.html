Temperature change from energy imbalance

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Foundations

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Relate an instantaneous energy imbalance to heating or cooling rate.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

An energy imbalance changes stored thermal energy. A nearly uniform-temperature body can have its instantaneous heating or cooling rate related directly to that imbalance.

Assumptions

Uniform body temperature at the instant of interest, approximately constant mass and specific heat, negligible phase change and other storage contributions. This is a basic energy balance, not the later transient-conduction solution methods.

Reasoning / derivation

With dU/dt=mcdT/dt, a cooling plate with no input obeys mcdTdt=−hAs(T−T∞). Include both exposed faces in area. Rearrange to infer h from the measured temperature-change rate. If radiation matters, include it in the balance as well.

Example

Week 7 Q3: m=3.75 kg, c=2770 J/(kgK), dT/dt=−0.022 K/s, temperature difference 200 K, two 0.3 m × 0.3 m faces. h=−3.75(2770)(−0.022)/[2(0.3)(0.3)(200)]=6.348 W/(m2K).
