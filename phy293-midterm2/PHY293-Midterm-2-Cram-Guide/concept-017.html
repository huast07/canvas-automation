de Broglie wavelength: slow particles

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Matter waves

Why this matters

Choose the massive-particle momentum before calculating wavelength.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · Massive particles also produce diffraction. The wavelength associated with momentum p is λ=h/p; it becomes observable when comparable with experimental dimensions.

Assumptions

Use p=mv=2mK only for nonrelativistic particles. For thermal neutrons PS7 uses K=3kBT/2.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · λ=h2mK. At fixed mass, quadrupling kinetic energy halves wavelength; at fixed energy, increasing mass reduces wavelength as 1/m. For a Bohr orbit, 2πr=nλ yields pr=nℏ, explaining the angular-momentum condition as a standing-wave condition.

Example

Level 3 · For an electron with K=13.6 eV, λ≃0.333nm. Dividing by the hydrogen Bohr radius gives roughly 2π, consistent with one wavelength fitting around the ground-state orbit.
Common trap: Do not use the photon formula E=hc/λ for a nonrelativistic massive particle.

PROBLEM TYPE

4. Matter-wave wavelength → diffraction angle

Level 1 · Massive particles also produce diffraction. The wavelength associated with momentum p is λ=h/p; it becomes observable when comparable with experimental dimensions.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · λ=h2mK. At fixed mass, quadrupling kinetic energy halves wavelength; at fixed energy, increasing mass reduces wavelength as 1/m. For a Bohr orbit, 2πr=nλ yields pr=nℏ, explaining the angular-momentum condition as a standing-wave condition.

Worked example

PS7 Q5 route: a thermal neutron at 300 K is diffracted by planes 2.82 Å apart. Find the first-order detector angle.

  1. K=3kBT/2≃6.21×10−21J.
  2. λ=h/2mnK≃1.45A˚.
  3. Bragg gives sinθ=1.45/(2×2.82), hence θ≈14.9 degrees. The detector angle asked for is 2θ≈29.8 degrees.
