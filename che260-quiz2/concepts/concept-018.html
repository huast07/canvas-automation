Radial temperature profiles and flux

CHE260 · Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer · Radial geometry

Unofficial study example · Fall 2026. This independently written guide is based on the posted CHE260 Quiz 2 scope. It is not an instructor-approved answer key. Quercus source links require course access. Worked examples are original unless identified otherwise.

Why this matters

Distinguish constant heat rate from changing radial flux and temperature slope.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Steady radial conduction conserves total heat rate through an unheated shell, while flux falls outward as the available area grows.

Assumptions

Constant k, steady radial flow, no shell generation. The formula does not describe temperature inside a heat-generating solid core.

Reasoning / derivation

For a cylinder, T(r)=Ti+(To−Ti)ln(r/ri)ln(ro/ri). For a sphere replace the logarithmic ratio by (1/ri−1/r)/(1/ri−1/ro). Thus the profiles are logarithmic and inverse-radius, not straight lines. Cylinder flux varies as 1/r; sphere flux as 1/r2.

Example

Original example: cylinder surfaces at 100°C and 0°C, with radii 1 and 4 cm. At 2 cm, the logarithmic fraction is ln2/ln4=1/2, giving 50°C. Flux at 4 cm is one quarter of flux at 1 cm.

PROBLEM TYPE

4 · Insulated cylindrical pipe

Steady radial conduction conserves total heat rate through an unheated shell, while flux falls outward as the available area grows.

Reasoning / derivation

For a cylinder, T(r)=Ti+(To−Ti)ln(r/ri)ln(ro/ri). For a sphere replace the logarithmic ratio by (1/ri−1/r)/(1/ri−1/ro). Thus the profiles are logarithmic and inverse-radius, not straight lines. Cylinder flux varies as 1/r; sphere flux as 1/r2.

Worked example

Original example: inner pipe surface at 70°C, insulation from radius 0.01 to 0.02 m with k=0.05 W/(m K), outer h=10 W/(m² K), air 20°C. Use 1 m length; no inner film or pipe resistance is needed because inner surface temperature is given.

  1. Rins=ln(2)/(2π×0.05×1)=2.20636 K/W.
  2. Ro=1/(10×2π×0.02×1)=0.795775 K/W.
  3. Q˙=(70−20)/(2.20636+0.795775)=16.6548 W, or 16.6548 W/m.
  4. Ts,o=20+16.6548(0.795775)=33.2534 ∘C. End temperatures bound the result.
