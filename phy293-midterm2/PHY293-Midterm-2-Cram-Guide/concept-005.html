Expectation values and symmetry

PHY293 · Waves and Modern Physics · Foundations

Why this matters

An expectation value is an ensemble average, not a guaranteed outcome.

Scope: confirmed

From first principles

Level 1 · For a discrete distribution, average each value with its probability. For a continuous distribution, replace the sum with an integral over probability density.

Assumptions

Normalize first. Symmetry shortcuts require symmetry of the density about the correct center, not merely a convenient sketch of the amplitude.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · ⟨x⟩=∫x|Ψ|2dx,⟨x2⟩=∫x2|Ψ|2dx. If ρ(c+u)=ρ(c−u), the odd contributions in ∫uρ(c+u)du cancel and ⟨x⟩=c.

Example

Level 3 · For Ψ=Ax(L−x) on [0,L], the density is symmetric about L/2. Thus ⟨x⟩=L/2 and left-half probability is 1/2, even though the density is not uniform.
Common trap: An average energy can lie between allowed measured energies.

PROBLEM TYPE

5. Normalize a shape and calculate probabilities

Level 1 · For a discrete distribution, average each value with its probability. For a continuous distribution, replace the sum with an integral over probability density.

Reasoning / derivation

Level 2 · ⟨x⟩=∫x|Ψ|2dx,⟨x2⟩=∫x2|Ψ|2dx. If ρ(c+u)=ρ(c−u), the odd contributions in ∫uρ(c+u)du cancel and ⟨x⟩=c.

Worked example

PS8 Q4: normalize ψ=Ax(L−x) on 0≤x≤L, then find left-half probability and mean position.

  1. ∫0Lx2(L−x)2dx=L5/30, so A=30/L5/2.
  2. The density is symmetric about L/2. Equal halves give P(0≤x≤L/2)=1/2.
  3. The same symmetry gives ⟨x⟩=L/2. This shape is admissible as a state but is not a single box energy eigenfunction.
